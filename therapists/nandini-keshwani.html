Approach

How she works

"A session with me is usually warm, collaborative, and non-judgmental. I aim to create a space where you can bring in whatever feels important, without feeling rushed or pressured."

Nandini works trauma-informed, drawing on humanistic therapy, CBT, and DBT depending on what's needed. Together, you make sense of your experiences at a pace that stays comfortable — not rushed toward a conclusion just because a session is running.

Modalities she uses: Trauma-informed therapy · Humanistic approach · CBT · DBT

Her story

How did she get into this work?

Growing up, Nandini watched her mother struggle with depression, and often felt helpless seeing someone she loved go through that much pain. That experience is what first sparked her curiosity about emotional wellbeing — and, eventually, about how people actually heal.

Outside of work, Nandini describes herself as pretty goofy — someone who romanticises the everyday and finds joy in small things. She's usually reading a psychological thriller, or occasionally a cheesy romance novel she won't quite defend, and spends a lot of time laughing and making memories with people she cares about.

Before you start

What she wishes people knew before starting therapy

"Therapy doesn't always have to look formal or clinical. Sometimes it simply looks like two people being as human as possible together."

You don't need the right words, a clear goal, or everything figured out before your first session. Nandini's aim is for you to be able to show up as yourself — whatever that looks like on a given day.

FAQ

Common questions

I'm not sure I have anything "serious enough" to see a therapist for.
You don't need to be in crisis to benefit from therapy. If something's been on your mind, or you've been going in circles, that's enough. A lot of the most useful work happens before things get to a breaking point.
What age group do you work with?
Primarily young adults and adults — roughly 18 to 40. I assess each enquiry individually, so if you're outside that range, feel free to reach out and we can discuss whether it's a good fit.
Do you offer sessions in Gujarati?
Yes. If Gujarati feels more natural for expressing certain things, that's completely fine. Sessions can be a mix of languages if that works better for you.
