Approach

How he works

Armeet trained in CBT for anxiety and personality disorders at the Beck Institute, and in REBT through In Vivo. DBT and ACT inform his work too, though he doesn't apply them rigidly — which one shows up in a session depends on what's actually in front of him.

Modalities he uses: CBT · REBT · DBT-informed · ACT-informed

In the room

What does a session with Armeet actually feel like?

Goal-directed and focused on the here and now — identifying thoughts, emotions, and behaviours, and working on wherever you're stuck.

The first few sessions tend to set the foundation — figuring out how your thoughts are actually getting in your way. He also uses metaphor and humour a fair amount; not every session needs to feel heavy to be doing something.

His story

How did he get into this work?

Armeet did his bachelor's in business. He was fairly lost after finishing it, and it was a friend who suggested he look into psychology — not a calling he'd had since childhood, just a suggestion he followed, and it stuck. He's also the facilitator of QueerKey, an offline mental health support group based in Pune.

Outside sessions, he's usually reading about geopolitics or talking about music. Health is something he pays close attention to for himself, and he spends a fair amount of time just watching people go about their day — which, unsurprisingly, tends to feed back into the work.

Before you start

What he wishes people knew before starting therapy

"It is almost never going to solve all your issues."

What it can do is help you understand what's interfering, and change your relationship to it. Armeet also likes to spend real time upfront on rapport — building the foundation of the working relationship before diving into the harder material.

FAQ

Common questions

I have OCD but I've heard therapy can make it worse. Is that true?
ERP (Exposure Response Prevention) is very effective for OCD when done carefully. I use a structured, evidence-based approach that is gradual and collaborative. Nothing happens without your consent and readiness.
Do you offer sessions in Punjabi?
Yes. If it's easier for you to express certain things in Punjabi, that's completely fine. We can mix languages within a session if that works better for you.
What's the difference between CBT, ACT, and REBT?
All three work with thoughts and behaviour, but in different ways. CBT challenges unhelpful thinking patterns. ACT focuses on accepting difficult feelings and committing to values-based action. REBT looks at the core beliefs that drive distress. I use all three — whichever fits what you're dealing with.
